Choosing an approach

This page maps biological mechanism questions and data structures to the right mediation estimand and CausalMediation API. If you only need the total causal effect of an exposure without decomposing pathways, use CausalTargeted.jl. If you are still deciding which covariates are confounders, mediators, or colliders, see CausalDynamics.jl.

What mediation analysis asks (in plain terms)

Suppose an exposure $A$ (for example, parasite load, diet, or vaccination) affects an outcome $Y$ (such as survival or fecundity), and you have measured an intermediate biological mechanism $M$ (such as an immune marker or hormone level). Mediation decomposes the Total Effect (TE) into two parts (Robins & Greenland, 1992; Pearl, 2001; VanderWeele, 2015):

  • Direct effect: the change in $Y$ when $A$ changes while the mediator $M$ is held constant (or drawn from its unshifted distribution).
  • Indirect (mediated) effect: the change in $Y$ attributable solely to how $A$ shifts the mediator $M$.

Simply adding $M$ to a regression of $Y$ on $A$ ("difference of coefficients") fails whenever there are nonlinearities, exposure–mediator interactions, or mediator–outcome confounders (VanderWeele, 2015).

The crucial biological question: is there an intermediate confounder (moc)?

In biology, exposures rarely affect only one intermediate trait. Often, an exposure $A$ alters another post-exposure variable $L$ (for example, body condition or feeding rate) that in turn affects both the mediator $M$ and the outcome $Y$:

W ──▶ A ──▶ L (moc) ──▶ M ──▶ Y
      │     └───────────────▲ │
      └─────────────────────┴─┘

Such a variable $L$ is called an exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounder (moc). Its presence determines which estimand is identifiable:

  • No intermediate confounder ($L$ absent): Natural direct and indirect effects (NaturalMediation) and organic effects (OrganicMediation) are identifiable under standard cross-world or organic assumptions (Pearl, 2001; Lok, 2015). Interventional mediation (InterventionalMediation) is also valid and agrees closely when $L$ is absent.
  • Intermediate confounder present ($L$ present): Natural direct and indirect effects are generally not identifiable without untestable cross-world assumptions (Avin, Shpitser & Pearl, 2005; VanderWeele, 2015). Use interventional (randomised intermediate) mediation (InterventionalMediation with moc = [:L]), which compares outcomes when $M$ is drawn from its population distribution under shifted vs unshifted $A$ given baseline covariates $W$ (Vansteelandt & Daniel, 2017; Díaz & Hejazi, 2020; Hejazi et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024). Alternatively, if you can scientifically conceive of setting $M$ to a specific constant value $m$, use the controlled direct effect (ControlledDirect; VanderWeele, 2015).

CausalMediation enforces this boundary: passing moc with NaturalMediation() is refused by assert_natural_admissible!, and the reserved RecantingTwinMediation type raises an explicit error rather than silently substituting an interventional effect.

Practical checks before interpreting mediation results

  1. Verify moc on your DAG first. Run identify(g, MediationQuery(...)) in CausalDynamics and pass the result to spec_from_identification so any intermediate confounder is detected automatically.
  2. Check nested Monte Carlo stability (mediation_n_mc_sweep). Under continuous shift policies, interventional mediation integrates over the mediator distribution via Monte Carlo (n_mc). Increase n_mc until point estimates and standard errors stabilise (Liu et al., 2024; see Getting started).
  3. Check sample size and weight truncation. Defaults reuse lean small-n Super Learner profiles from CausalTargeted. For static discrete arms, uncapped EIF inference requires weight_truncation = false; see Methods and literature.

Minimal example: mediation with an intermediate confounder

using CausalMediation, CausalTargeted, StableRNGs

# Synthetic cohort: baseline W, exposure A, intermediate confounder L (moc),
# mediator M, outcome Y
df, _ = simulate_intermediate_confounding_mediation(250; rng = StableRNG(301))

spec = MediationSpec(
    :A, :Y;
    mediators = [:M],
    covariates = [:W],
    moc = [:L],
    effect = InterventionalMediation(),
)

res = run_mediation(
    spec, df;
    deltas = [-0.5, 0.0, 0.5],
    folds = 2,
    n_mc = 16,
    estimator = :onestep,
    learners = SMALL_N_SL_LEARNERS,
    parallel = false,
    rng = StableRNG(302),
)

res.table[:, [:delta, :estimand, :est, :se]]
