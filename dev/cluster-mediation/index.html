Clustered mediation

ClusterMediationSpec and run_cluster_mediation provide an experimental frequentist parametric reference for the canonical Theorem 2 EIF estimator of Ham, Cheng and Li (2026). This page also documents the separate experimental parametric hierarchical Bayesian route (fit_bayesian_cluster_mediation and bayesian_mediation_effects), which reuses ClusterMediationSpec for the same targets. Neither route is part of the ordinary MediationSpec / run_mediation API.

Daeyoung Ham, Chao Cheng and Fan Li (2026). Semiparametric causal mediation analysis in cluster-randomized trials with post-treatment confounding. arXiv:2611.11903v1, 9 November 2026. Paper · doi:10.48550/arXiv.2611.11903. The shared book bibliography key is ham2026cluster.

Equations (1)–(4) define the targets, Table 1 defines the auxiliary means, Theorem 2 and equations (5)–(7) define the canonical scores, and Appendix I.2 (pp. 53–54) describes the optional weighted intercept fluctuation below.

This implementation is written independently from the published equations; the authors' R source is neither translated nor distributed. Their repository's public licensing terms were pending when this implementation was prepared.

Target and assumptions

Treatment $A$ is assigned to independent clusters by Bernoulli randomisation with known common probability $π$. Each member has a measured post-treatment confounder $Z_j$, continuous mediator $M_j$ and outcome $Y_j$. Baseline information $C$ includes the supplied covariates; $N$ is cluster size. Outcomes may depend on other members' post-treatment variables, with no interference across clusters.

Let $G(a′)$ be a joint mediator draw under arm $a′$, conditional on $C,N$, independent of the confounder vector $Z(a)$ retained under the outcome arm:

\[\theta(a,a′)=\frac{E\left[\frac{W}{N}\sum_{j=1}^N \iint\eta_j(a,z,m,C,N)\phi(z\mid a,C,N) \kappa(m\mid a′,C,N)\,dz\,dm\right]}{E[W]}.\]

For $τ$, replace $κ(m|a′,C,N)$ by $κ_j(m_j|1,C,N)κ_{-j}(m_{-j}|0,C,N)$ separately for each member $j$. This removes own/peer mediator dependence. Individual and spillover contrasts therefore include dependence changes as well as marginal distribution changes; they are not unique allocations to causal pathways. The member-specific mixed interventions need not form one implementable assignment to the entire cluster.

te uses a separate inverse-randomisation-weighted score. It need not equal stochastic_total. weighting=:cluster sets $W=1$; :individual sets $W=N$. Cluster membership and size must be determined before treatment.

Identification requires consistency, adequate measured control of joint mediator-outcome confounding, and joint positivity under the specified stochastic interventions. Randomisation alone does not establish mediator-outcome exchangeability. The runner records identification=:user_assumed and does not consume the existing no-interference MediationQuery certificate.

Model and equations in code

Within each training arm, marginal means for $Z,M$ are linear in cluster size, member baselines and cluster-mean baselines. Joint residual covariance is

\[\operatorname{Cov}\begin{pmatrix}Z\\M\end{pmatrix} =\Sigma_{\mathrm{within}}\otimes I_N+ \Sigma_{\mathrm{between}}\otimes\mathbf1_N\mathbf1_N^T.\]

The two 2-by-2 components are estimated from member and distinct-peer residual cross-products and projected onto the positive semidefinite cone. Within-component eigenvalues are bounded below by $10^{-8}$ for numerical evaluation. This Gaussian random-intercept reference excludes negative intracluster correlations and arbitrary dependence. Conditional Gaussian algebra yields $ξ(M|A,Z,C,N)$; marginalisation yields $ϕ(Z|A,C,N)$ and $κ(M|A,C,N)$.

Arm-specific outcome regression uses the full quadratic polynomial in size, member and mean baselines, and own/leave-one-out peer $Z,M$ summaries. Before forming quadratic monomials, outcome inputs are centred and scaled using training-fold moments. Their Gaussian means and covariances undergo the same affine transformation during integration. This preserves the quadratic function space while preventing a change of confounder origin from causing numerical rank loss. Least squares uses a pseudoinverse for structural duplicate columns. Baseline covariates must be complete, numeric and suitably scaled for the linear process models. Singleton peer summaries are zero.

_cluster_u and _cluster_r implement Table 1's integrated means. _cluster_quadratic_mean uses $E[x_jx_k]=μ_jμ_k+Σ_{jk}$ for exact integration, including covariance terms. _cluster_θ_score follows equation (5):

\[\psi_\theta=\frac{W}{N}\sum_j\left[ \frac{I(A=a)}{\pi_a}\{\omega^{(1)}(Y_j-\eta_j)+u_{1j}-u_{3j}\} +\frac{I(A=a′)}{\pi_{a′}}(u_{2j}-u_{3j})+u_{3j}\right],\]

where $π_a=π^a(1-π)^{1-a}$ and $ω^{(1)}=κ(M|a′,C,N)/ξ(M|a,Z,C,N)$. _cluster_τ_score follows equations (6)–(7). Ratios use log densities and are evaluated only on the residual's assigned arm. Off-arm residual terms remain exactly zero.

All members stay in their cluster's treatment-stratified fold. The ratio estimator and cluster influence curve are

\[\widehat\theta=\frac{\mathbb P_K\widehat\psi_\theta}{\mathbb P_KW}, \qquad \widehat D_i=\frac{\widehat\psi_{\theta,i}-W_i\widehat\theta}{\mathbb P_KW}.\]

Linear contrasts are applied to joint influence curves before their sample covariance is divided by $K$. This preserves component covariance and the individual-average normalisation. Intervals use a Student $t_{K-1}$ critical value as a finite-cluster convention, not an exact small-sample result.

Optional weighted intercept fluctuation

stabilise=true implements Ham et al.'s Appendix I.2 adjustment. For each validation fold and each of $θ(0,0),θ(1,0),θ(1,1),τ$, define the weighted outcome residual contribution $R_i$ and residual weight $H_i$:

\[R_i=\frac{W_i}{N_i}\sum_j h_{ij}(Y_{ij}-\widehat\eta_{ij}),\qquad H_i=\frac{W_i}{N_i}\sum_j h_{ij},\qquad \widehat\epsilon=\frac{\sum_{i\in I_r}R_i}{\sum_{i\in I_r}H_i}.\]

The member weight $h_{ij}$ includes the arm indicator, inverse randomisation probability and mediator density ratio. Shift the outcome regression and all compatible integrated means by the same $ε$. Their differences are unchanged, so the score update is directly

\[\widehat\psi_i^{\,*}=\widehat\psi_i+(W_i-H_i)\widehat\epsilon.\]

This solves $\sum_{i\in I_r}(R_i-H_i\widehat\epsilon)=0$ without recomputing the integrals or changing the target. Cluster-average and individual-average fits use different fluctuations. The adjustment changes finite-sample point estimates; diagnostics expose fold_fluctuations and fold_residual_sums. The ordinary trial te is unchanged. stabilise=false retains the original reference route.

This option is separate from ratio_cap. Capped fits still withhold uncertainty. Uncapped stabilised intervals require the appropriate nuisance and normalisation rates; solving the residual equation does not establish interval validity under misspecification or inadequate overlap. The authors omit a detailed asymptotic justification of this adjustment in Section 6, referring to earlier work.

The corresponding R routines are ap_stabilization_epsilon, ap_score_theta_fold and ap_score_tau_fold. On 32 shared score fixtures spanning both weighting schemes, θ/τ kernels and stabilisation settings, maximum absolute score discrepancy was $4.09\times10^{-14}$ and fluctuation discrepancy $3.61\times10^{-16}$. This is score-level concordance, not validation of fitted nuisances or their complete study. The reference file's Git blob SHA was f162da6239e03709ea42870c25e1f147aa9c7c3b.

Inference and verification limits

Uncapped fits return inference_status=:asymptotic. EIF uncertainty requires correct nuisance limits, suitable convergence rates, finite score moments and adequate overlap; cross-fitting alone does not guarantee nominal coverage. The general paper's multiple-robustness claims are not a guarantee for arbitrary misspecification of this constrained parametric backend.

A finite ratio_cap≥1 caps ratios to $[1/cap,cap]$ for point fits and sets :truncated_weights, withholding standard errors, covariance, influence curves and limits for the whole result. Uncapped overflow or underflow throws rather than silently changing weights.

Inspect diagnostics.min_log_ratio, max_log_ratio and capped_fraction. The extrema describe active density ratios before capping. Off-arm residual ratios are not evaluated. These diagnostics describe numerical overlap; they do not establish the causal positivity assumption.

Initial calibration, 13 November 2026

The CDCS driver scripts/stress_harness/run_cluster_mediation_reference.jl ran 400 replications at each of 100 and 300 clusters under Julia 1.13.3, using the example's exact Gaussian DGP and treatment-dependent $Z\times M$ outcome interaction. Assignment probability was 0.5, weighting was cluster-average, cross-fitting used three folds, and ratios were uncapped. Replication $r$ at $K$ clusters used MersenneTwister(10_000K + r).

These results establish a limitation of this uncapped reference, not a comparison with the authors' stabilised implementation. The estimator is not ready for routine inferential use. Agreement with the analytic integrals and score equations does not establish stable fitted estimation or calibrated intervals. Further overlap diagnostics, justified stabilisation and independent replicated validation are needed before promoting it beyond reference use.

Implementation comparison and ablation

The authors' canonical code uses the weighted intercept fluctuation above, ratios bounded to $[0.05,20]$, a selected outcome basis and common residual covariance parameters for each fitted process across treatment arms. It derives the parametric marginal mediator law from its fitted $M|Z$ and $Z$ laws. Our Gaussian joint law already preserves that compatibility through Gaussian marginalisation and conditioning. Pooling covariance or reducing the outcome basis would impose additional modelling restrictions and has not been adopted silently. Their exchangeable-density formulas offer a future optimisation; our current dense calculations provide a more general reference.

The authors' interval convention is $\sqrt{K/(K-3)}t_{K-3}$ applied to their population-variance SE, whereas this reference uses sample covariance with $t_{K-1}$. This small critical-value difference has not been used to tune away the observed calibration problem. See their shared core (ap_fit_process, ap_derive_compatible_kappa, ap_effect_rows; Git blob SHA c4c326dfc4a24b94aa4fc436527729309c985ad3).

For the uncapped adjusted route, IDE bias was 0.00137 at K=100 and -0.00161 at K=300 (Monte Carlo SEs 0.00381 and 0.00173). Point stability improved substantially, but interval coverage remained inadequate. The adjustment is therefore opt-in, not a claim of repaired inference. Capped routes retain the package's unavailable-uncertainty contract; their coverage is undefined, not zero. These results concern our own DGP and backend, not the authors' reported simulation performance. Further work must examine the variance of the actual stabilised estimator and validate coverage before recommending its intervals.

Centering and scaling the polynomial inputs preserved these original-scale raw results. A regression test additionally verifies that adding $10^6$ to the confounder's measurement origin preserves estimates and SEs; the previous unscaled polynomial fit failed that test.

Tests cover analytic intervention integrals, nonlinear recovery, score terms, singleton reduction, weighting, joint covariance and invalid inputs. They do not establish end-to-end fitted concordance with the authors' R engine. Further calibration and that comparison remain necessary before routine use.

The existing MediationSpec moc path uses a different confounder-mediator coupling. This API does not replace it or enable its unavailable uncertainty. Flexible learners, the low-dimensional classification estimator, missingness and longitudinal mediation remain outside this reference implementation.

Hierarchical Bayesian clustered mediation

fit_bayesian_cluster_mediation and bayesian_mediation_effects provide an experimental parametric Bayesian alternative for the same clustered targets (te, stochastic_total, ide, iie, iime, and isme), reusing ClusterMediationSpec (weighting and π metadata) while remaining separate from the frequentist run_cluster_mediation Theorem 2 EIF reference. Rather than forming inverse-probability and mediator density ratios, this route models the joint law parametrically and propagates parameter uncertainty from the posterior. It is not a semiparametric or doubly robust EIF estimator: it trades density-ratio instability for explicit parametric mechanism forms, Gaussian random-intercept assumptions, and declared prior specifications. Causal identification remains :user_assumed.

The model factorises across three one-level random-intercept Gaussian mechanisms:

  • Post-treatment confounder $p(Z \mid A, C, N)$ linear in $[1, A, N, C_j, \bar{C}]$.
  • Mediator $p(M \mid A, Z, C, N)$ linear in $[1, A, N, C_j, \bar{C}, Z_j, \bar{Z}_{-j}]$.
  • Outcome $p(Y \mid A, Z, M, C, N)$ using $[\phi_d(x_{kj}), A_k \phi_d(x_{kj})]$, where $\phi_d$ is the linear (outcome_degree=1) or quadratic (outcome_degree=2, default) polynomial in $[N, C_j, \bar{C}, Z_j, \bar{Z}_{-j}, M_j, \bar{M}_{-j}]$.

Each mechanism is fitted via CausalTargeted's structured variational engine $q(\beta, b)\,q(\sigma_\varepsilon^{-2})\,q(\sigma_b^{-2})$ (Nolan, Menictas & Wand 2020) with exact Schur-complement block algebra and exact affine design reparameterisation. Query cluster_mediation_design_dimensions(spec; outcome_degree) to construct explicit ClusterGaussianPrior entries for :confounder, :mediator, and :outcome; no outcome-derived prior scaling is performed.

For each shared draw from the three fitted mechanism posteriors, bayesian_mediation_effects integrates analytically over the outcome random intercept and over $(Z_k, M_k)$ using exact $O(1)$ exchangeable-covariance algebra (without allocating $N_k \times N_k$ cluster matrices), returning joint posterior means, SDs, credible intervals, MCSEs, and joint covariance under :cluster or :individual weighting. If any mechanism fit has status !== :converged, intervals and joint covariance are withheld (NaN / intervals_available = false).

Loading PSIS (using PSIS, activating CausalTargetedPSISExt) enables correction = :psis, which applies Pareto-smoothed importance sampling (Vehtari et al. 2024) across the joint global parameter draws. Operational gates (khat < 0.7, ess >= 400, and mcse / sd <= 0.05 across non-degenerate effects) withhold intervals and covariance (NaN / intervals_available = false) on failure without silently falling back to raw variational intervals.

using CausalMediation, CausalTargeted, LinearAlgebra, PSIS

dims = cluster_mediation_design_dimensions(spec; outcome_degree = 2)
mkprior(p) = ClusterGaussianPrior(
    mean = zeros(p),
    covariance = 4.0 * Matrix{Float64}(I, p, p),
    residual_shape = 3.0,
    residual_scale = 2.0,
    cluster_shape = 3.0,
    cluster_scale = 1.0,
)
priors = (
    confounder = mkprior(dims.confounder),
    mediator = mkprior(dims.mediator),
    outcome = mkprior(dims.outcome),
)

fit = fit_bayesian_cluster_mediation(spec, train_df; priors, outcome_degree = 2)
res = bayesian_mediation_effects(fit, target_df; correction = :psis)
res.estimates
